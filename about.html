The Interstitial Lag: Why Mentors Matter

One of the hardest things for newly diagnosed patients to understand is that Continuous Glucose Monitors (CGMs) do not measure blood directly; they measure interstitial fluid (the fluid between your cells).

Because glucose takes time to travel from the bloodstream into the interstitial fluid, your CGM reading is actually 5 to 15 minutes "behind" your actual blood sugar. When your blood sugar is rising or falling rapidly, this lag can cause immense confusion and dangerous over-corrections. Endocrinologists explain the science, but peer mentors teach you the real-world timing required to pre-bolus your insulin and beat the lag.

Breaking the Diabetes Distress Cycle

Diabetes is as much a psychological battle as it is a physiological one. Constantly calculating carbs, fighting insurance companies, and waking up to alarms takes a massive toll known as "Diabetes Distress."

When distress turns into full burnout, Time in Range plummets. Our program focuses heavily on the mental aspect of the disease. Your peer mentor isn't just a data analyst; they are someone who understands exactly how exhausting it feels when you do everything "right" and your blood sugar still spikes. We help you rebuild your relationship with your data so you can manage your health without sacrificing your mental well-being.
